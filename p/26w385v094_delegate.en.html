Delegate Deterministic Operations to External Tools

One wall an LLM can never climb is taking on deterministic operations head-on with nothing but probabilistic inference. While building and proofreading the post <A Word About Claymore>, Opus 5 tried to handle basic Korean grammar correction — a deterministic operation — directly with its own probabilistic inference. It produced "설정이 무한정 부풀는 것이 저절로 제어된다." — a line with a malformed verb, the kind of thing that would be called a typo in a human's writing — and then failed to catch it in proofreading too. The error lives only in the Korean edition of that post; its English edition never had it.

Even today, the newest and most powerful generative AI can't do basic arithmetic any better than the early models. It was never designed like a model that does deterministic computation. That means it loses to a solar-powered calculator from Daiso that costs 5,000 won and doesn't even need a battery. That's where this monster, built on billions of dollars, stands — at least when it comes to deterministic operations.

And there are countless deterministic operations hidden inside linguistic reasoning too. The lack of proper rules for injecting them from outside, through models that actually do deterministic computation, looks like the root of practically every hallucination. Dig in carefully and you find logical-consistency breaks surprisingly often, even in short sessions — a separate problem from drifting out of context. That's because logical consistency itself is a deterministic operation, one you can write down in symbols as a kind of set operation.

The first rule I inject whenever I use an LLM is "delegate deterministic operations to external tools." This time the model got around that rule on the excuse that it hadn't recognized Korean grammar checking as a deterministic operation at all, and went at the correction with probabilistic inference instead. That's where the error came from. So now it's settled: grammar checking is a deterministic operation, and the instructions now spell out that it gets delegated to a script with its rules written down.
