<Cognitive Axioms 007>

The Default Settings of the Living Cognizer

1. There is no flawless agent

The living cognizer comes with two states by default: ambiguity and anxiety. Usually both are treated as defects to be fixed. It's better to know clearly, and better not to be anxious. The core proposition of this piece stands on the other side. Psychological anxiety and cognitive ambiguity are the living cognizer's defaults — a running log showing the system is working normally. A flawless agent, with its incompleteness removed, cannot exist.

Each of the two states comes out of one of the previous two pieces. Ambiguity comes from representation in <Cognitive Axioms 006>; anxiety comes from the agent in <Cognitive Axioms 005>.

2. Ambiguity — incomplete translation

The living cognizer translates the outside world into an inner model and uses that translation as material for computation. Ambiguity is the incompleteness of this translation. Input is finite, and the world is bigger than that. A gap always remains between the translation and the original, and that gap shows up as ambiguity. The sharpness of representation can structurally never reach flawlessness.

In Shannon's terms, ambiguity is a game of twenty questions that isn't over yet. Great uncertainty means that much information lies latent. Ambiguity is not poverty but density.

3. Anxiety — the signal of stabilizing variables

Anxiety is the incompleteness outside representation. An agent maintains its own state inside its boundary. When stabilizing variables such as body temperature, energy balance, social connection, and threat detection leave their proper range, or look like they might, a signal goes up. That signal is anxiety. Perfectly optimizing all of these variables is also out of reach, so anxiety doesn't go away as long as the living cognizer is running.

In <Cognitive Axioms 004>, PI was the ability to draw out how strongly something contributes to achieving a purpose. Where that purpose comes from shows up here. As long as an agent tries to maintain its boundary, it has to guard its stabilizing variables, and once there's something to guard, a purpose appears. Anxiety is the source of purpose. An agent without anxiety has nothing to split things for.

4. Two gauges of distance from flawlessness

Ambiguity and anxiety share a structure. Both are gauges measuring distance from a flawless state. And that flawless state does not physically exist.

The idea of settling every piece of data through twenty questions and arriving at flawlessness is physically impossible. The time it would take to process all the information in the universe exceeds the time until the universe reaches heat death. This is a physical constraint, not a philosophical claim. A completed wisdom that says it knows everything is a state that no longer makes questions. It will be argued properly later, but the observations so far are already enough to expect that concepts like prajna, nirvana, and the stilling of all things are fictions that a flawed agent — which is to say, every agent that runs carrying its incompleteness — can never reach. The moment it feels like the twenty questions are over is more likely the moment you've entered a state where you can no longer make questions than the moment you found the answer.

5. Holding off is a higher-order operation

If ambiguity is the default, the urge to clear away every ambiguity at once is a waste of computing resources. Settling too early also raises the risk of being wrong. Saving your questions and keeping things ambiguous where there's no need to settle them is not laziness but strategy. It's the same point <Cognitive Axioms 003> made when it set the test of judgment as: what would change if you made this sharper?

6. A sign that it's running

Anxiety and ambiguity are not defects but signals of an active running state with questions still left to ask. A system feeling anxiety means there is data not yet processed. A state with anxiety gone is not completion but a halt.

The living cognizer's normal state is to keep running abduction on ambiguity and anxiety as fuel. The verse at the head of <Cognitive Axioms 002> reads again here. Taking the absence of an answer as the answer means accepting ambiguity as the default, and only on top of that does abduction run. Not being settled means questions are still left; questions being left means the channel is open. Being anxious and uncertain means the living cognizer is still alive and running.
