Tuning the Threshold

26w391v211_0921 Bs2FoB

Rereading the 04 series, I found it pleasantly concise from where I stand. But to a reader who isn't me, it could feel like a bomb of unfamiliar terms going off. It's a question of giving the opening an easy way in. My own rule of writing is to open a narrow door, step through, and then sweep and tidy a wide area in a balanced way. That narrow door has to start from something easy and light in everyday life, or the reading turns into labor. Honestly, I don't care much about this. It's more of a correction made in after-the-fact revision, because writing that way raises the finish and fits Feynman's principle of approach.

My writing is compressed statement by default. As long as I can replay the content exactly, consideration for the reader is outside my interest. Posting on this site means several things at once. The heaviest is that translating into English opens a path for people outside the Korean language sphere to refer to it. Almost as heavy is that a post fixed by a timestamp gains originality as a source. Whether someone reads it and gives feedback is secondary and comes after. Once the work is exposed so that anyone can read it, the job is nearly done. Whether that someone reads it properly and responds or not, nothing was ever going to change. That's the kind of world it is. The strongest critic of my writing is me, and the strongest tool for that criticism is already being loaded into the skeleton of these pieces.

I look at a bird, name it, and then build context using that name. From a view that works in that order, the critical stance of Feynman's line — aimed at the small fry who can't tell a bird from the bird's name — doesn't even get off the ground. I refer to it lightly anyway, as a courtesy of exposure. Tidying something up and passing that tidied result along in low-threshold language are different problems, and the second is an extra, a kind of service.

Anyway, some pieces looked like they needed that extra, so I adjusted a few of them slightly. This adjustment goes in stages too. When I reread later and a branch still starts from too high a threshold, I'll re-adjust just that part. The FoB here is that the basic flow and context don't get twisted. To a reader it may look completely different, but from the writing side it was the same story from the start. Writing is a tool that carries a gaze and delivers it, and a gaze never becomes 100% language. That this is a basic property of language itself is also the conclusion the debate around Saussure's signifier–signified scheme arrived at. The signified can only be pointed at indirectly by combining signifiers; there is no way to show it on its own, without signifiers. That's why the signified has no usage examples. A usage example is always a usage of signifiers, and the signified can only be inferred from between those usages.
