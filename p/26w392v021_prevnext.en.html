Previous and Next Buttons

26w392v021_0922 Bs2FoB

As posts started to pile up, navigation became a problem. To go from one piece to the previous or next one in the same tab, you had to return to the list and click the entry there. A needlessly clumsy routine. So I put buttons at the bottom of each post that take you straight to the previous and next pieces in that tab.

In the Log and Snap tabs, where the newest entry comes first, the next post sits on the left and the previous one on the right, so the buttons match what the eye expects. In the Verse and Writing tabs, which open with piece number one and grow in order, the layout is reversed. This, too, came from actually putting the buttons in, clicking through, and removing whatever felt off.

It's a fix for a problem I ran into while posting “A Story About Salt” in the Snap tab, solved right where it showed up. There's plenty of talk online, on YouTube especially, about food containers, salt, additives and colorings. Trace it carefully, though, and much of it turns out to be junk: thin on evidence or with the weights skewed. Pseudoscience, and further down anti-science nonsense, is not rare either. I see no reason to single any of it out and hit it. But the irritation I feel when I see it is hard to shake. Why do they do that? If you don't know, just say you don't know.

Follow the spread of junk knowledge that hardens bias and locks in misreadings, and it almost always leads back to someone's interest. Sometimes directly, sometimes indirectly, and often in ways that look unconnected yet end up serving whoever spreads it. Researching the salt piece also shifted my view a little on canned tuna, Spam, and burgers and pizza. For me that's enough. I don't see a need to dig further. Holding off is a higher-order operation.

After adding the buttons, I tested the site and found that the existing Index button didn't actually open the list. It was built to step back to the previous page in the browser history, and under the new way of moving between posts, that blind spot became a bug. So I changed the logic itself. Opus 5 didn't catch this on its own, and in the end admitted it couldn't have caught it through its own analysis. The nonsense that a site gets built in a single click keeps getting hammered at small points like this one. Coding is no different.
