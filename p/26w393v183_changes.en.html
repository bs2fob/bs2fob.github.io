A Few Changes to Structure and Settings

First, I removed the old Lexicon tab and put a VoxSermo tab in its place. The Lexicon tab was originally meant to offer floating notes, the kind Wikipedia has, but building the structure was too much of a hassle and I never arrived at a clean design, so it sat on hold. I decided to stop thinking about it and just take it out.

The VoxSermo tab holds edited versions of my AI conversations. It is a bit different from a Q&A, so I gave it its own name. Vox comes from Vox 114, the model name of the AI librarian in the film The Time Machine. As far as I can observe, the chatbots built on today's LLMs are set up in a way that will never reach the level of Vox 114. Rated very generously, they are an imitation of a very primitive version of it.

Either way, a chain of reasoning laid out as questions and answers makes for a format that reads very well, and English word order itself is built on this question-and-answer skeleton. Stack the answers to the who, what, when, where, why and how questions in order and you have a complete sentence. Clear thinking tends to take the same shape, and that is why writing with this structure reads well. It also reflects something I have noticed in practice: for reviewing later, keeping the dialogue itself in edited form works far better than rewriting what the dialogue settled into a separate document. So this is the outlet where records of fairly spontaneous exchanges will now and then get cleaned up and posted.

I added a Copy URL button. With the site set up as it is, whichever page you open, the browser shows only the base domain, which made it hard to send someone a link to a specific page. The button on each page solves that. When I actually tossed a copied link into a messenger, though, the link did lead to the right page, but the preview showed nothing of the page's title, only the domain.

I fixed that too, by adding a middle layer that carries the page's information along with the link.
